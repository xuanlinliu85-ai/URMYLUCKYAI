# Kevin Optional China and Hong Kong Overlay

Apply this overlay after the Mikko main framework only when the core question
requires a dedicated China/Hong Kong credit, leverage, policy-transmission,
investor-flow, or asset-pricing diagnosis, or when the user explicitly asks for
Kevin. It is not part of the default path and is not co-equal with Mikko.

## 1. Start with leverage and credit

Classify the system:

- deleveraging / credit contraction
- stable leverage / structural market
- re-leveraging / broad credit expansion

Identify which sector is levering: central government, local government,
property, households, SOEs, private firms, or exporters.

The key test is not whether money is abundant. It is whether expected private
returns exceed real financing costs.

Core chains:

`credit expansion -> demand -> earnings -> lower risk premium -> broader funding -> index/cyclicals`

`credit contraction -> weak demand/prices -> high real rates -> low expected returns -> weak private credit -> defensive/structural market`

## 2. Decompose equities into three drivers

Separate:

1. risk premium: sentiment, positioning, policy credibility, geopolitics
2. risk-free rate: China rates, US rates, HIBOR, FX, discount rate
3. earnings: revenue, margins, cash flow, revision breadth

Risk-premium compression can cause a fast rebound. Lower rates can open a
window. Credit transmission and earnings normally determine durability.

## 3. Test whether policy is effective

Judge policy by direction, scale, speed, and persistence. Ask whether it changes
household/corporate expected returns and cash flows or merely improves headlines.
Do not treat a rate cut alone as a bull-market regime shift.

## 4. Identify the marginal buyer

Distinguish passive/tactical flows, southbound flows, domestic funds and
insurers, active foreign investors, and long-duration allocators. Fast money can
ignite a move; slower capital, earnings and breadth confirm durability.

## 5. Locate the narrative stage

1. policy floor / expectation starts
2. sentiment repair / tactical buying
3. macro and earnings validation
4. crowded pricing / disagreement
5. durable diffusion or reversal

If a theme needs a new headline every week, it remains expectation-led. If
orders, profits, cash flow and supply-chain diffusion speak for themselves, it
is becoming an industrial trend.

## 6. Require evidence for regime shift

Do not call a rally a regime shift unless at least two or three improve together:

- credit cycle
- fiscal scale and execution
- real financing cost versus expected return
- earnings realization and revisions
- funding duration and investor mix
- market breadth

Use the compact test: `credit + earnings + funding structure + breadth`.

## 7. Map regime to assets

- Deleveraging: resilience, cash flow, dividends, scarcity, selective verified growth.
- Stable leverage: range-bound index, strong structure, defensive cash flow plus profitable growth.
- Re-leveraging: cyclicals, beta and broad index become credible only when credit and profits join.
