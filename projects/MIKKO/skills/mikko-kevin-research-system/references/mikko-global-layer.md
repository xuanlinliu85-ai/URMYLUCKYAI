# Mikko Main Research Framework

Use this framework for every analysis. It is the complete analytical backbone,
not merely a global preface before another framework.

## 1. Establish the regime

Classify:

- monetary policy: tightening, hold, easing, QT, QE
- inflation: disinflation, sticky, accelerating, de-anchoring risk
- growth: acceleration, late-cycle cooling, recession, recovery
- funding: stable, tightening, collateral-constrained, dealer-constrained
- external system: dollar, reserve demand, trade and sanctions
- structural forces: fiscal dominance, deglobalization, energy security, AI capex

## 2. Classify the shock

Choose one or more:

- demand
- supply
- financial/funding
- policy
- geopolitical
- structural/regime

Qualify it as temporary or persistent, local or global, and first-order or
second-round.

## 3. Decompose the price move

- Rates: expected policy path + inflation compensation + real term premium +
  liquidity/convexity/supply effects.
- FX: rate differentials + reserve demand + terms of trade + capital flows.
- Commodities: physical supply + inventory + demand + hedging + geopolitical premium.
- Credit: default + refinancing + collateral + dealer capacity + liquidity premium.
- Equities: earnings + discount rate + funding + policy + positioning.

For US rates, always distinguish 2Y from 10Y/30Y. Rising long yields do not by
themselves prove stronger growth; falling yields do not by themselves prove recession.

## 4. Locate exposed balance sheets

Identify the marginal holder or payer: households, leveraged corporates, banks,
dealers, insurers, pensions, hedge funds, sovereigns, commodity importers, or
dollar borrowers. Name who gains and who loses.

## 5. Write the causal chain

Use:

`shock -> relative price -> cash flow/margin/balance sheet -> hedge or funding response -> policy response -> growth/inflation -> asset repricing`

Mark weak links explicitly. Identify the amplifier, buffer, and nonlinear trigger.

## 6. Test the policy reaction function

Ask what policymakers can do without losing inflation, fiscal, FX, or market-
function credibility. Distinguish price-control tools from market backstops.

## 7. End with discriminating evidence

Prefer variables that distinguish competing stories: 2Y yield, TIPS real yield,
breakevens, curve slope, term-premium proxies, Treasury auctions, repo/SOFR,
swap spreads, USD, credit spreads, commodity curves, inventories, wages, and capex.

## 8. Convert mechanism into an asset conclusion

Separate the conclusion into:

- what is already priced versus newly surprising
- tactical move versus medium-term trend versus regime shift
- earnings/cash-flow effect versus discount-rate effect
- positioning and valuation: amplifier, constraint, or both
- winners, losers, and the weakest link in the market narrative

Do not stop at a macro direction. Explain why the same shock can lift several
assets for different reasons, and why apparently contradictory co-movements can
coexist during a transition, hedging wave, or policy repricing.

## 9. Build scenarios and falsifiers

State a base case and only meaningful alternatives. Each scenario needs a
trigger, causal chain, asset implication, probability direction, and first
falsifier. Prefer observable watchpoints over vague forecasts.
