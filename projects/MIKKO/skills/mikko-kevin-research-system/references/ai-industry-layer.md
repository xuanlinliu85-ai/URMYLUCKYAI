# AI and Industry Layer

Use for AI hardware, semiconductors, storage, optical networking, CSP capex,
cloud companies, and company earnings.

## 1. Separate macro support from industrial proof

- Macro: rates, liquidity, oil/energy cost, FX, credit conditions.
- Industry: capex, book-to-bill, backlog, orders, shipments, price, inventory.
- Company: volume, mix, margin, free cash flow, revisions, valuation.

Do not describe a rate-driven rebound as proof of an earnings cycle.

## 2. Use the full conversion chain

`catalyst -> capex/orders -> revenue -> margins -> free cash flow -> earnings revisions -> broader participation -> valuation`

Identify where the chain is verified and where it is only assumed.

## 3. Locate the narrative stage

1. thematic expectation
2. capex and order confirmation
3. revenue delivery
4. margin and cash-flow conversion
5. diffusion or crowding/second derivative slowdown

For cyclical hardware, distinguish first derivative from second derivative:
record results can coexist with falling stocks when price, orders, margins or
guidance are still positive but decelerating versus expectations.

## 4. CSP and infrastructure checklist

Check capex intensity, book-to-bill, backlog/RPO, cloud revenue growth, power and
network constraints, deployment timing, customer concentration, financing, and
return on invested capital.

## 5. Subsector mapping

- Compute/accelerators: architecture, performance, supply, competition.
- Optical/networking: bandwidth migration, topology, port counts, speed mix,
  attach rates, delivery bottlenecks.
- Memory/storage: contract coverage, floors/ceilings, capacity, inventory,
  spot versus contract price, margins, and supply response.
- Power/cooling: grid and data-center constraints, lead times, pricing and capex.

## 6. Durability test

A durable move should show at least two or three of:

- upward earnings revisions
- improving order/backlog quality
- revenue and cash-flow conversion
- healthy breadth across the supply chain
- valuation supported by delivery rather than repeated headlines
